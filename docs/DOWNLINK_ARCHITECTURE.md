# A UAS video downlink: ADRV9002 + fabric modem

Status: DESIGN. Not scheduled, not started. Written so implementation does not
begin with a week of re-derivation, and so the decisions below can be argued
with rather than rediscovered.

Companion to `AGENTS.md` (toolchain reality) and
`designs/libresdr_radio/top.ex` (the AD9363 front end as built).

**Read this first:** the PHY is the easy part. An OFDM modem at these rates is
textbook and there is an open reference (`openwifi`) running one on this class
of part. The three things that will actually decide whether this works are the
**digital interface rate**, the **latency ledger**, and the **ground-station
antenna** — in that order. Sections 2, 7 and 8. Everything else is arithmetic.


## 0. The one-paragraph summary

Target is an O4-class link: 5.8 GHz, ~10 km, sub-30 ms glass-to-glass, video
downlink with a control uplink. The transceiver is an **ADRV9002** (30 MHz –
6 GHz, 2×2, up to 40 MHz bandwidth, integrated DPD, fast frequency hopping).
The modem is OFDM in fabric with a **fixed 240 kHz subcarrier spacing and a
scalable FFT** (N = 64/128/256 for 10/20/40 MHz), an asymmetric TDD frame, and
adaptive MCS closed on uplink SNR reports. The airborne PA targets +27 to
+30 dBm conducted with MCS-linked adaptive backoff; **range is bought with
ground-station antenna gain, not airborne watts**. The AD9363 LibreSDR stays the
waveform-development vehicle at 2.4 GHz; the ADRV9002 enters at the product
stage, and not before.


## 1. Why the ADRV9002, and what it costs

Chosen over the alternatives on RF performance and mission fit:

| Part | Top freq | Max BW | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| AD9363 (have it) | 3.8 GHz | 20 MHz | **Cannot reach 5.8 GHz.** Lab only. |
| LMS7002M | 3.8 GHz | 120 MHz | **Cannot reach 5.8 GHz.** Ruled out. |
| AD9361 | 6 GHz | 56 MHz | Viable. More BW, no DPD, no fast hopping. |
| **ADRV9002** | **6 GHz** | **40 MHz** | **Chosen.** TDD-native, DPD, hopping, low power. |

The ADRV9002 is built for exactly this mission — TDD tactical radio, battery
budget, frequency agility — and the integrated DPD is worth real money in PA
backoff (§7). Multichip sync and fast frequency hopping map directly onto the
frequency-agility behaviour that makes DJI's link feel robust.

Two costs, both real:

- **40 MHz is the ceiling.** O4's 60 MHz mode is unreachable with this part.
  Section 5 shows this does not bind: 40 MHz at 64-QAM 3/4 already yields
  ~143 Mbps, above O4 Pro's 130 Mbps headline. We are not bandwidth-limited, we
  are link-budget-limited, which is the correct place to be limited.
- **The control plane is not a register list.** The AD9363 is driven from Elixir
  by hand-assembling 24-bit SPI transactions against a byte-pipe master, and
  that was the right call for it. The ADRV9002 is configured by kilobyte-scale
  profile blobs generated by ADI's tooling, behind a large C API. **Do not port
  that API.** See §6.


## 2. The interface is the real constraint

The ADRV9002 talks to the fabric over SSI, and the mode determines everything:

| Mode | Lanes | Max lane rate | Max clock | Max sample rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CSSI (CMOS) | 1 | 80–160 MHz | 80–160 MHz | 2.5–5 MSPS |
| CSSI (CMOS) | 4 | 80–160 MHz | 80–160 MHz | 10–20 MSPS |
| LSSI (LVDS) | 1 | 983.04 Mbps | 491.52 MHz | 30.72 MSPS |
| LSSI (LVDS) | 2 | 983.04 Mbps | 491.52 MHz | 61.44 MSPS |

This table is the fork in the whole project.

**LSSI at ~1 Gbps/lane cannot be captured in fabric flops.** It needs
ISERDES/OSERDES, and per `AGENTS.md` the ILOGIC input flip-flop path is dead
under openXC7 — 2 of 14 ILOGICE3 site muxes characterised in prjxray, and the
missing twelve are precisely the input selectors. The fabric-DDR workaround that
rescued the AD9363 at 16 MHz DATA_CLK does not scale by a factor of thirty. So:

> **The 40 MHz product mode requires either Vivado or a fixed prjxray. There is
> no third option.** This is a decision to make deliberately, not to discover
> during bring-up.

**CSSI 4-lane is the vendor-free escape hatch**, and it is a genuinely useful
one: 10–20 MSPS at an 80–160 MHz clock is a parallel CMOS bus, capturable with
the same fabric-DDR technique already proven on the AD9363 — at a higher clock,
but within reach. That supports the 10 MHz mode outright and the 20 MHz mode
marginally. It is enough to bring up a real waveform on real silicon with no
vendor tools anywhere, which is a result worth having on its own terms.

**Design consequence:** the SSI front end is a parameterised component with two
physical implementations behind one internal sample-stream interface. The modem
above it never learns which is underneath.

```
Hw.ADRV9002.SSI.CMOS   — 4-lane CSSI, fabric DDR capture
Hw.ADRV9002.SSI.LVDS   — 2-lane LSSI, ISERDES/OSERDES
Hw.ADRV9002.SSI        — mode select, presents Hw.Interface.SampleStream
```


## 3. The channel is not WiFi's channel

Air-to-ground at altitude is benign in the dimension WiFi optimises for and
hostile in one it mostly ignores:

- **Delay spread is small.** Strong LOS plus ground bounce, typically well under
  200 ns excess delay. There is no rich indoor multipath to fight.
- **Doppler is significant.** At 5.8 GHz: 580 Hz at 30 m/s, 871 Hz at 45 m/s,
  1548 Hz at 80 m/s.

802.11's 312.5 kHz spacing and 800 ns CP are tuned for the opposite channel and
spend ~20% of the symbol on a guard interval that buys nothing here.

**Choose 240 kHz spacing and a CP of N/16.** At 240 kHz, the worst Doppler case
above is 0.64% of a subcarrier — ICI is negligible. A 260 ns CP covers a 78 m
path difference, which comfortably exceeds realistic A2G excess delay, at
**5.9% overhead instead of 20%.**

Make the CP a per-mode parameter, not a constant. `CP = N/8` (520 ns, 156 m,
11.1% overhead) is the robustness fallback for terminal-area and near-ground
operation where the ground bounce geometry gets worse, and it should be
selectable without a rebuild.


## 4. Fixed spacing, scalable FFT

This is the load-bearing design principle, borrowed from LTE rather than 802.11.

| Mode | fs | N | Used | Data | Occupied | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 MHz | 15.36 MSPS | 64 | 42 | 38 | 10.08 MHz | 4.427 µs |
| 20 MHz | 30.72 MSPS | 128 | 84 | 76 | 20.16 MHz | 4.427 µs |
| 40 MHz | 61.44 MSPS | 256 | 168 | 156 | 40.32 MHz | 4.427 µs |

Spacing is 240 kHz in every mode. Useful symbol is 4.167 µs in every mode. CP is
260 ns in every mode. **Symbol rate is 225.9 ksym/s in every mode.**

The payoff is that sync, CP removal, pilot tracking, equalization, the frame
timer and the entire MAC are *bandwidth-invariant*. Changing channel width
changes the FFT size and the subcarrier map and nothing else. In EHDL terms this
is one `param :N_FFT` and a generated subcarrier table — the rest of the design
does not know which mode it is in.

It also means the 10 MHz mode developed on the LibreSDR is the *same waveform*
as the 40 MHz product mode, not a scale model of it.


## 5. Throughput, and why we are not bandwidth-limited

At 90% downlink duty:

| Mode | QPSK 1/2 | 16QAM 1/2 | 64QAM 3/4 | 64QAM 3/4 2×2 | 256QAM 5/6 2×2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 MHz | 7.7 | 15.5 | 34.8 | 69.5 | 103.0 |
| 20 MHz | 15.5 | 30.9 | 69.5 | 139.1 | 206.0 |
| 40 MHz | 31.7 | 63.4 | 142.7 | 285.4 | 422.9 |

(Mbps, before FEC framing overhead and pilots already excluded.)

Read the QPSK 1/2 column, not the 256-QAM one. **The range mode is the product.**
1080p60 H.265 at 8–12 Mbps fits inside 20 MHz QPSK 1/2 with margin to spare, and
that is the mode that has to work at 10 km. The high-MCS columns exist to be
walked *down* from at close range, and they show there is enough headroom to
spend on stronger FEC rather than chasing spectral efficiency.


## 6. Frame structure and the MAC

- **2 ms TDD frame**, 451.8 symbols. 90/10 downlink/uplink → 407 DL, 45 UL.
  A 1 ms frame halves worst-case queuing latency at the cost of doubling
  preamble/guard overhead; make it a parameter and measure.
- **Uplink at QPSK 1/2 in 20 MHz yields 1.72 Mbps** — ample for RC control,
  telemetry, and per-frame link feedback.
- **No ARQ on video.** Propagation at 10 km is 67 µs; the round trip through
  frame boundaries is milliseconds. Systematic FEC sized to the frame instead,
  with an outer erasure code across the video block (WFB-NG's insight, and it is
  correct). Reserve HARQ for the control link, where a 5 ms stall is invisible.
- **Inner FEC: convolutional K=7 + Viterbi for v1.** Cheap, well understood,
  ~2 dB from LDPC, and `openwifi` has a reference implementation to check
  against. LDPC is a v2 upgrade, not a v1 requirement.
- **Adaptive MCS closed on post-equalizer SNR reported in the uplink slot**,
  with hysteresis and asymmetric ramp rates — drop fast, climb slow. This single
  loop determines usable range more than any other choice in the system, and it
  is mostly policy, which means it is cheap to iterate on.

### Control plane: Linux/IIO on the PS, not a fabric SPI engine

ADI maintains an IIO kernel driver for the ADRV9002. On a Zynq running Nerves,
the right split is:

- **PS/Linux** owns transceiver configuration: profile load, calibration, gain
  tables, hopping tables, DPD adaptation. Elixir talks to it over libiio.
- **Fabric** owns the datapath and the frame timer: SSI, modem, FEC, MAC timing.
- **The boundary is a sample stream plus a small AXI control/status surface**,
  the same shape `libresdr_radio` already has.

This preserves the Elixir story where it is valuable — policy, orchestration,
test — without reimplementing tens of thousands of lines of vendor API in it.
The hand-rolled SPI approach was right for the AD9363 and does not generalise.


## 7. Power: the number is not 1 W

No transceiver IC delivers 1 W. The ADRV9002, like the AD9361 and LMS7002M, puts
out single-digit dBm. Reaching +30 dBm is an external driver-plus-final chain,
which is a PCB subsystem with its own bias, matching, filtering and TDD gating.

The cost driver is not average power, it is **PAPR**. OFDM runs 10–12 dB raw,
7–8 dB after crest-factor reduction. Delivering +30 dBm *average* at 10 dB
backoff needs a PA with P1dB near +40 dBm — a 10 W device at 10–20% efficiency,
so **5–10 W dissipated on an airframe**. That is a heatsink, airflow, and
endurance you do not get back.

Two mitigations, one of which is why we chose this part:

- **The ADRV9002's integrated DPD** recovers 3–6 dB of backoff. *Open question:*
  confirm whether its observation path costs an RX channel in 2T2R (§10).
- **MCS-linked adaptive backoff.** QPSK 1/2 tolerates roughly −10 to −13 dB EVM;
  256-QAM needs about −32 dB. The long-range mode can run near compression at
  5–6 dB backoff and far better efficiency; only short-range high-MCS modes need
  deep backoff — **and at short range the power is not needed.** Power and
  linearity requirements are anti-correlated with range. The system never needs
  1 W and −32 dB EVM in the same instant, and one that knows this is cheaper,
  cooler and lighter than one that does not.

**Regulatory ceiling.** FCC 15.247 caps 5725–5850 MHz at 1 W conducted, with
conducted power reduced dB-for-dB for antenna gain above 6 dBi. The generous
exception — higher gain with no power reduction — applies only to *fixed*
point-to-point operation, which an aircraft is definitionally not. Beyond 1 W
means an amateur licence (5.65–5.925 GHz is a ham band, but that means a
licensed operator and no encryption) or a Part 90 / experimental path. Decide
early; it constrains the product, not just the prototype.

### Buy range on the ground, not in the air

Link budget at 5.8 GHz, 1 W conducted, +3 dBi airborne, 20 MHz, 5 dB NF:

| Range | 8 dBi ground | 16 dBi ground |
|---|---|---|
| 1 km | 29.3 dB | 37.3 dB |
| 5 km | 15.3 dB | 23.3 dB |
| 10 km | 9.3 dB | 17.3 dB |
| 20 km | 3.3 dB | 11.3 dB |
| 25 km | 1.3 dB | 9.3 dB |

QPSK 1/2 wants 5–8 dB. **10 km closes with a few dB of margin on a modest ground
antenna; 25 km closes on a good one.**

Doubling airborne PA power is +3 dB for several watts of DC, real mass, and a
thermal problem in flight. Going 8 dBi → 16 dBi on the ground is **+8 dB for
zero grams and zero airborne watts**, and it helps the uplink too. This is what
DJI actually does — the O4 air unit is a few grams and nowhere near 1 W; their
15 km comes from receive-side gain, diversity, and an MCS loop that walks
gracefully down to a few Mbps.


## 8. The latency ledger is the spec

15–30 ms glass-to-glass covers sensor readout → ISP → encode → packetize → FEC →
air → deFEC → depacketize → decode → display. **The radio is 3–5 ms of that.**

DJI hits 15 ms because they own every stage and no stage has a buffer boundary
in it. Most open FPV systems bolt a competent radio onto a standard encoder and
a Linux networking stack, land at 80–120 ms, and feel nothing like O4 regardless
of PHY quality.

**Every architectural decision gets tested against the ledger before anything
else.** Write it down as a table of stage budgets, populate it with measurements
as stages come up, and treat a stage that overruns as a design defect rather
than a tuning problem.

This is also where EHDL's actual leverage is, and it is not "another OFDM
modem." It is that encode → FEC → frame → modulate can be **one pipeline with no
buffer boundaries and no OS in the path**, with the MCS loop closing in fabric in
microseconds instead of through a driver, a bus, and a scheduler. That is
precisely what WFB-NG cannot do, because it sits behind a COTS WiFi MAC it does
not control. It is a real and defensible difference — and it is a *systems*
argument, not a PHY argument.


## 9. Staged path, and the rule about changing one thing

**The rule: do not change transceiver, band, and toolchain at the same time.**
Every hard-won finding in `AGENTS.md` came from being able to attribute a
failure to one variable.

- **Stage 0 — fix the simulator.** Non-negotiable precondition, and unrelated to
  clocks or ISERDES. At 4 ticks/s one OFDM symbol is 2.7 minutes and sync
  development is impossible; `AGENTS.md` already documents three occasions where
  the instrument became the device under test. The `_top_` per-edge fix (items 1
  and 2 there) looks like most of the win.
- **Stage 1 — waveform on the LibreSDR.** AD9363, 2.4 GHz, 10 MHz mode, existing
  fabric-DDR front end at existing rates. Prove sync, channel estimation,
  equalization, the frame timer, and the MCS loop against a modelled channel and
  then over a bench cable. Band and part unchanged from what already works.
- **Stage 2 — ADRV9002 on CSSI.** New part, same band, same 10 MHz waveform,
  vendor-free flow intact. Isolates the transceiver and its control plane.
- **Stage 3 — 5.8 GHz and LSSI.** New band, new interface, and the Vivado /
  prjxray decision from §2 comes due. Isolates the interface.
- **Stage 4 — product board.** PA chain, antennas, thermal, codec integration.

### Product-board FPGA, and the tension worth naming

The obvious product part is **Zynq UltraScale+ EV (ZU4/5/7EV)** — a hardened
H.264/H.265 VCU on-die puts codec and modem in the same chip with no bus between
them, which is exactly what §8 demands. It is architecturally the O4-class
answer.

It is also Vivado-only: prjxray and openXC7 are 7-series. The alternative keeps
the codec off-die (dedicated low-latency encoder, or an OpenIPC-class SoC) on a
larger 7-series part, preserving the vendor-free stance at the cost of a bus
crossing in the one place the latency argument says we cannot afford one.

The honest framing: **vendor-free is a research commitment that has earned real
findings — the ILOGIC gap, the CMT corrections, the fabric-DDR technique — and
it should not be allowed to dictate the product's architecture.** EHDL stays the
language and the 7-series flow stays the research vehicle either way.


## 10. Open questions

Not answered here, and each one can move a decision:

1. **Does ADRV9002 DPD cost an RX channel in 2T2R?** The observation path needs
   a receiver. If it consumes one of the two, receive diversity and 2×2 MIMO are
   in tension with DPD, and §7's backoff argument changes.
2. **CSSI 4-lane at 160 MHz in 7-series fabric** — does fabric DDR capture close
   timing without ILOGIC? At 80 MHz almost certainly; at 160 MHz unknown. This
   sets whether Stage 2 gets the 20 MHz mode or only the 10 MHz mode.
3. **ADRV9002 power consumption in 2T2R wideband**, measured, not from the
   marketing page. Feeds the airframe energy budget alongside the PA.
4. **Realistic A2G excess delay** for the intended flight profiles. §3 asserts
   <200 ns from general principles; a measurement decides between CP = N/16 and
   N/8, worth 5% of throughput.
5. **Fabric budget for the 40 MHz modem** — 256-pt FFT, Viterbi, equalizer,
   2×2 — against `-nocarry`, on whichever part Stage 4 lands on. `openwifi`'s
   utilisation on its target is the cheapest available estimate.
6. **Encryption and the licence path.** If the amateur route is taken to exceed
   1 W, encryption is not permitted — which may be disqualifying for the product
   regardless of the RF benefit.
